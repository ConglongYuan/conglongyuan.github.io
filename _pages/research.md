    <div>
      <p class="studio-label">Research</p>
      <h1>From soft order to optical function.</h1>
    </div>
    <p class="studio-page-head__intro">My work asks how molecular design, elasticity, defects, and external fields select a soft structure—and what becomes possible once that structure is controllable.</p>
  </header>

  <section class="studio-section studio-wrap research-logic" aria-labelledby="logic-title">
    <blockquote id="logic-title">A device is most durable when its function follows from a well-understood physical state.</blockquote>
    <div class="research-logic__body">
      <p>I approach soft photonics as a connected chain rather than as separate materials and device problems. The starting point is molecular or mixture design. The middle is an elastic landscape in which phase, helix, defect, and boundary compete. The end is an optical response that can be tuned, patterned, or stabilized.</p>
      <p>This logic keeps the evidence readable: identify the structure, determine what controls it, then connect that mechanism to the measured function.</p>
      <ol class="logic-chain" aria-label="Research logic">
        <li><span>01</span><b>Material</b></li>
        <li><span>02</span><b>Elasticity</b></li>
        <li><span>03</span><b>Architecture</b></li>
        <li><span>04</span><b>Control</b></li>
        <li><span>05</span><b>Function</b></li>
      </ol>
    </div>
  </section>

  <div class="studio-wrap" aria-label="Research territories">
    <section class="territory" aria-labelledby="territory-soft">
      <div class="territory__index">01 / Core</div>
      <div>
        <div class="territory__lead">
          <div class="territory__copy">
            <p class="studio-label">Elastic physics of soft matter</p>
            <h2 id="territory-soft">Which ordered state does a soft material choose—and why?</h2>
            <p class="territory__question">The central problem is selection: among many nearby structures, which state is stabilized by molecular geometry, elastic competition, confinement, and field?</p>
            <p class="territory__body">Liquid crystals make this problem visible. Helicoidal, heliconical, twist-bend, and defect-rich states respond strongly to small changes in composition or boundary conditions. I use that sensitivity to study phase evolution and stability, while keeping the causal chain from material to structure explicit.</p>
          </div>
          <figure class="territory__visual">
            <img src="/images/research/story-thermo.webp" alt="Published heliconical structures at different optical responses" width="1200" height="750">
            <figcaption>Published heliconical architectures under coupled field and temperature control. Cropped and recomposed from Advanced Materials (2025).</figcaption>
          </figure>
        </div>
        <div class="territory__details">
          <div><h3>Mechanism</h3><p>Competing elastic modes, molecular packing, phase transitions, confinement, and field-induced reorientation.</p></div>
          <div><h3>Selected work</h3><p>Soft-helix transformation; frequency-actuated heliconical states; room-temperature-stable twist-bend and heliconical materials.</p></div>
          <div><h3>Questions now</h3><ul><li>How can soft ordered states remain tunable without becoming transient?</li><li>When do defects become stable structural units?</li></ul></div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="territory" aria-labelledby="territory-photonics">
      <div class="territory__index">02 / Photonics</div>
      <div>
        <div class="territory__lead">
          <div class="territory__copy">
            <p class="studio-label">Optical-field control & communications</p>
            <h2 id="territory-photonics">How can a reconfigurable soft structure shape an optical field?</h2>
            <p class="territory__question">The aim is not simply tunability, but a physical route from controlled architecture to wavelength, polarization, phase, or spatial-mode output.</p>
            <p class="territory__body">This territory connects liquid-crystal helices, patterned interfaces, holographic processing, and light-responsive films to optical functions. Published examples include tunable structural color, polarization-dependent information display, diffractive optical elements, and reconfigurable surface reliefs.</p>
          </div>
          <figure class="territory__visual">
            <img src="/images/research/story-stability.webp" alt="Published polarization-dependent optical device concept and multicolor output" width="1200" height="750">
            <figcaption>A stable heliconical architecture connected to polarization-dependent optical functions. Cropped and recomposed from Matter (2023).</figcaption>
          </figure>
        </div>
        <div class="territory__details">
          <div><h3>Mechanism</h3><p>Field-dependent helix geometry, polarization selectivity, patterned retardation, diffractive surfaces, and photo-induced deformation.</p></div>
          <div><h3>Selected work</h3><p>Hyper-stable heliconical photonics; dynamic soft lenses; programmed holographic reliefs; liquid-crystal holographic devices.</p></div>
          <div><h3>Questions now</h3><ul><li>Which degrees of freedom can be controlled independently?</li><li>How should structured light and soft matter share the task of modulation?</li></ul></div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

            <p class="studio-label">Biomedical optics & instrumentation</p>
            <h2 id="territory-biomedical">What optical information can an instrument recover without adding unnecessary complexity?</h2>
            <p class="territory__question">This is an emerging, instrument-oriented direction—not a claim of a mature biomedical platform.</p>
            <p class="territory__body">The working focus is on computational optical imaging, label-free contrast, and optical manipulation. The connection to the established research spine is methodological: control an optical field, understand the interaction, and build a measurement that exposes the relevant information.</p>
